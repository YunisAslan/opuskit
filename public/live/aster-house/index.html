Shikhov, on the Caspian

Twelve housescut into the cliffabove the Caspian.Each one is builtaround the lightof a single hour.Twelve houses cut into the cliffabove the Caspian. Each one isbuilt around the light of a singlehour of the day.

Leyla Gadirli drew the row so that no two houses share an hour. Dawn takes the sunrise, Noon takes the sun overhead, Night looks across the bay to the lights of Baku. One release of twelve, finished in spring 2027.

The hours

Each house has one room set to catch its hour: a window cut at the right angle, a wall left bare to hold the light. These are those rooms, two houses to an hour.

  • 05:40 and 06:00, facing east

    Dawn and Six

    The first two houses take the sunrise over the sea. Their bedrooms are on the east wall, so the day starts with light on the stone, not an alarm.

    See Dawn

  • 08:00 and 10:00, facing south-east

    Eight and Ten

    Kitchen hours. Window frames print themselves across the plaster and walk slowly down the wall while the coffee is made.

    See Ten

  • 12:00 and 14:00, facing south

    Noon and Two

    The brightest houses keep the sun overhead. A glass roof over the hall takes the light straight down the middle, and the rooms either side stay cool.

    See Two

  • 16:00 and 17:20, facing south-west

    Four and Golden

    A slab of afternoon light crosses the long stone wall and holds there. Golden, the largest house, keeps it until the hour before sunset.

    See Four

  • 18:00 and 19:30, facing west

    Six Evening and Dusk

    Low, warm sun reaches the back wall of the living room. The terraces are set lower here, so the sunset stays in view from the sofa.

    See Six Evening

  • 20:00 and 22:00, facing north-west

    Blue Hour and Night

    The last two houses turn to the bay. After the sun has gone, one deep window keeps the blue, and later the lights of Baku come on across the water.

    See Night

On the rock

  • The row from the air

    The houses step down the limestone in pairs, each turned a few degrees from its neighbour to face its own hour.

  • Leyla Gadirli, architect

    “I didn’t design twelve houses. I designed twelve windows and built a house behind each one. The sun does the rest, at the same time every day.”

  • The lower terraces

    Every house has a stone terrace at sea level and a plunge pool fed with filtered sea water.

  • Parapet, Blue Hour

    Walls are the cliff’s own limestone, quarried during the dig and laid back in place.

  • The stair to the sea rooms

    Inside, one stair runs down through the rock from the living floor to the bedrooms by the water.

  • A sea room

    The lowest rooms open straight onto the water. You hear the Caspian before you see it.
