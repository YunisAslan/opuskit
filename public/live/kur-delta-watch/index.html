The river so far

People in Neftchala over sixty remember a delta loud with birds. This is what happened since, oldest first.

  1. 1974

    A delta full of birds

    The winter count logs more than 90,000 waterbirds between the river mouth and the Gizilagach bay: pelicans, flamingos, herons in every reed bed.

  2. 1993

    Less water reaches the sea

    Dams and irrigation upstream take more of the Kür every year. In dry summers the river arrives slow and shallow, and the side channels silt up.

  3. 2014

    The sea comes up the river

    In a low-water August, Caspian salt water pushes kilometres upstream. Reed beds along the lower channels die back for two seasons.

  4. 2018

    Rubbish settles where the current slows

    Bottles, bags and net floats from upstream towns gather in the reeds and on the bends. A fisherman counts 300 bottles on 100 metres of bank.

  5. 2019

    The watch starts

    On 12 October, twelve neighbours and one borrowed boat spend a Saturday on the south bank. They weigh what they pull out: 1.8 tonnes.

  6. 2020

    The first water test

    Six sampling points, the first Sunday of every month, a lab in Baku. Every result since has been published, good or bad.

  7. 2023

    Herons nest at the old fish ponds

    Grey herons raise young in the reeds by the abandoned ponds for the first time in at least ten years.

  8. 2026

    2,140 tonnes out

    Counted to 30 September. Forty-one pelicans on the north channel this autumn.

What we do

  • Cleanup Saturdays

    April to November, on the banks and in the reeds. Every sack weighed.

  • The monthly water test

    Six fixed points, one lab, every result published.

  • Boat days

    Two boats for the channels you can’t reach on foot.

  • Bird counts

    Spring and autumn, the same route every time.

  • School visits

    A lesson in class, then an afternoon on the bank.
