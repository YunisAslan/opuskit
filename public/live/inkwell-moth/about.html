About Nell

I draw the small animals people walk past, and give them a reason to stay on the page.

Nell Arden has written and illustrated picture books since 2016, when The Moths Who Came to Tea found a publisher. A year later she moved her table into an old bakery in Sheki, under the hills, where the bread ovens used to be.

Every page starts in pencil, goes down in ink with a dip pen and is coloured in watercolour by hand. Nothing is painted on a screen; the computer only scans what the table made.

How a book gets made

The same four steps for every book, whoever wrote it. A 32-page picture book takes about six months at the table.

  1. 1

    The pencil rough

    Every spread is drawn small and fast in pencil, with the words pasted beside it, so the author and editor can see the whole book in an afternoon.

    3 to 4 weeks

  2. 2

    The ink

    Once the roughs are agreed, each page is redrawn full size with a dip pen. This is where the animals get their faces.

    6 to 8 weeks

  3. 3

    The colour

    Watercolour in thin washes, left to dry overnight between layers. Mistakes stay; sometimes they become the best part of the page.

    8 to 10 weeks

  4. 4

    The pages laid out

    The paintings are scanned at the studio, set with the text and sent to print with a colour proof Nell checks by eye, page by page.

    2 weeks
