I’m Ines Varga. I draw maps by hand, in ink and watercolour, for towns, trails and books.

Before that I spent six years drawing plans in a surveyor’s office, which taught me accuracy and made me miss the pencil. Now every map starts with a walk and ends with an original sheet you can hang. I work alone, take a few commissions a year, and answer my own email.

How a map gets made

The walk

I go there. I walk the streets or the trail with a notebook and mark what you notice on foot: the hill, the shade, the turn people miss. A book map starts the same way, only the walk is through the manuscript.

The pencil

Back at the desk I sketch the whole sheet in soft pencil. This is where things move: a street gets wider because it matters, a hill gets taller because it hurts.

The ink

Then the ink, one pen at a time, and no going back. The lettering goes in last, by hand, so every name sits where it reads best.

The colour

Watercolour comes last and stays quiet: a wash for water, one for parks, the path a little louder. Then the sheet is scanned, and the original is yours.

How I got here

Dates are the real ones; the reasons are the honest ones.

  1. 2012

    Plans in a surveyor’s office

    Six years of exact lines. It taught me scale, and how little of a place a plan shows.

  2. 2018

    A map for a walking club

    Drawn for free, at weekends. Forty copies, and the first time strangers used something I drew.

  3. 2020

    Went on my own

    One desk, one pen, a list of three towns to ask.

  4. 2022

    The first trail on public boards

    Six boards around a lake. Proof that hand-drawn can survive the weather.

  5. 2025

    The first book map

    An archipelago that doesn’t exist, which turned out to need more surveying than a real one.

  6. Now: 2026

    Commissions open

    Taking a few maps a year, booked in order. Yours could be next.
