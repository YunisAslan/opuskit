Made of six thingsMade of six things

No screens, no lights, no app to install. Just the parts that make a keyboard good to type on, done properly.

  • Machined aluminium case

    One block of 6063 aluminium, powder-coated fern green. It weighs 1.6 kg, so it never walks across the desk.

  • A 6.5° angle, built in

    The slope is part of the case. No feet to fold out, nothing to break off.

  • Hot-swap sockets

    Five-pin sockets take almost any MX-style switch. Pull one out, push one in.

  • 65%, arrows kept

    67 keys: no number row lost, no arrow keys hidden behind a layer.

  • Double-shot PBT keycaps

    Cream letters, grey modifiers. The legends are moulded in, so they never wear off.

  • The Halvik Dial

    A sixteen-key pad with three knobs, sold with the keyboard or on its own.

From box to typing in ten minutesFrom box to typing in ten minutes

  1. Step 1

    Pick your switches

    Choose linear, tactile or silent, or pull them all and fit your own.

  2. Step 2

    Plug in USB-C

    It works on Mac, Windows and Linux at once. One switch on the back sets the modifier keys.

  3. Step 3

    Remap in the browser

    Open the Halvik page in Chrome or Edge to remap any key or knob. Nothing to install.

Up close

Every edge is rounded and bead-blasted before coating, so nothing feels sharp under your palms. Under the caps, a polycarbonate plate and two layers of foam keep the sound low and soft.
