Programs

Pick the one that suits you. Most volunteers end up doing two.

  • Replanting dives

    Saturday mornings, March to May. Tie seedlings to the reef in pairs, with a dive lead. You need your own qualification and a dry suit.

  • Beach days

    Sundays, all year. Sort, seed and count on the shore. No diving and no experience needed.

  • Nursery evenings

    Wednesdays, October to February. Clean tanks, check lines and watch the seedlings grow.

  • The autumn count

    Three weeks in September. Snorkel or dive the planted reefs and count every plant.

  • Shore school

    Weekday mornings in term. Rock-pool lessons for local classes, led by trained volunteers.

A year in the life of one plant

From a ripe blade on a wild plant to a counted plant on the reef, one year.

The line has reachedthe water

  1. 1One tide

    Collect

    On a low tide in October we take a few ripe blades from wild plants, never the whole plant.

  2. 2Six weeks

    Seed

    Spores settle onto twine wrapped around tubes in the nursery tanks, cold and dim like the seabed.

  3. 3December to February

    Grow

    The seeded lines hang in sheltered water off the quay until the seedlings are a finger long.

  4. 4March to May

    Outplant

    Divers carry the lines to the reef and tie them to the rock, a metre at a time.

  5. 5Three weeks

    Count

    In September we swim back and count every plant. The ones that hold are the forest.
