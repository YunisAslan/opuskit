Ways to join in

  • Replanting dives

    Tie seedlings to the reef with a buddy and a dive lead. Qualified divers only.

  • Beach days

    Sort, seed and count on the shore. No diving, no experience, wellies welcome.

  • Nursery evenings

    Tend the seeded lines in the tanks on Wednesday evenings, all winter.

  • The autumn count

    Snorkel or dive the planted reefs and count every plant, one by one.

  • Shore school

    Rock-pool mornings for local classes, led by our volunteers.

A forestyou cannot seefrom the shoreA forest you cannotsee from the shore

From the quay at Skerra Bay the water looks empty. Ten metres down it used to be a forest: kelp taller than a house, swaying in the swell, full of fish, crabs and the young of everything that lives on this coast.

Storms, warmer summers and urchins took most of it in a single generation. When three of us dived the point in 2018, we found a few stands holding on, and a lot of bare rock.

So we started small. A few wild plants, a borrowed shed, twine and tanks. Spores settle on the twine, grow into seedlings over the winter, and in spring divers tie them to the reef. Every autumn we count them, by hand, one by one.

Beach days start again on Sunday 2 November. No diving needed.

Bring warm layers. We bring the tea and the gloves.
