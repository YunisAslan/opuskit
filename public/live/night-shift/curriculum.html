// Curriculum// Curriculum

Eight weeks in the suite

Each week has a technique, a real shot to grade and a hand-in. Weeks 1 to 6 use the course shots so everyone grades the same frames; weeks 7 and 8 use a scene from the library.

// 01 Weeks// 01 Weeks

What each week covers

Two live sessions a week: Tuesday teaches the technique on the week’s shot, Thursday reviews every student’s grade of it.

  • // 01 Ingest// 01 IngestWeek 1

    Set up to see

    A grade is only as good as the screen you judge it on. You set up the room, the monitor and the signal path, then bring in the course footage the way a post house does.

    1. 1.1Grey walls, low lamps and why they matter
    2. 1.2Calibrating the monitor you already own
    3. 1.3Colour management from camera log to display
    4. 1.4Ingest and a project that holds together
    The shot
    Night street, camera log
    Hand in
    A photo of your setup and its calibration report

  • // 02 Scopes// 02 ScopesWeek 2

    Read the signal

    Before you touch a wheel, you learn to read what the image is doing: where the light sits, which channel runs hot, where skin falls.

    1. 2.1Waveform: levels and exposure
    2. 2.2RGB parade: finding the cast
    3. 2.3Vectorscope: hue, saturation and the skin line
    4. 2.4False colour on set and in the suite
    The shot
    Night street, camera log
    Hand in
    Scope readings of three frames, with notes and no grading

  • // 03 Balance// 03 BalanceWeek 3

    Lift, gamma, gain

    Primary correction on the night street: set the black and white points, take the sodium cast out of the shadows, and match two angles of the same corner.

    1. 3.1What each wheel actually moves
    2. 3.2Black point, white point, then everything else
    3. 3.3Neutralising mixed street light
    4. 3.4Matching shot to shot on the scopes
    The shot
    Night street, two angles
    Hand in
    A balanced, matched pair of shots

  • // 04 Contrast// 04 ContrastWeek 4

    Curves and density

    Contrast decides where the eye goes. You build it with curves rather than saturation, and keep the night dark without crushing it.

    1. 4.1Custom curves, and the S you shouldn’t draw
    2. 4.2Density without a LUT
    3. 4.3Highlight roll-off on practical lights
    4. 4.4Noise, and when to stop pushing
    The shot
    Night street, graded
    Hand in
    The night street, finished, with your node tree

  • // 05 Skin// 05 SkinWeek 5

    Faces first

    The golden-hour portrait. Skin of every complexion sits on one line of the vectorscope; you learn to put it there and still keep the backlit sky.

    1. 5.1The skin line and how to read it
    2. 5.2Qualifying skin without the plastic look
    3. 5.3Holding a sky behind a backlit face
    4. 5.4Warmth that belongs to the light
    The shot
    Golden-hour portrait
    Hand in
    The portrait, graded, with skin on the vectorscope before and after

  • // 06 Mixed light// 06 Mixed lightWeek 6

    Two colours of light

    A dim room lit by a caged bulb and a window. You separate the sources with windows and keys, then decide which one the scene belongs to.

    1. 6.1Power windows and tracking
    2. 6.2Hue-versus-hue and hue-versus-sat curves
    3. 6.3Keys that survive movement
    4. 6.4Choosing what reads as warm
    The shot
    Room, lamp and window
    Hand in
    The room graded two ways, with a note on which is right

  • // 07 Look// 07 LookWeek 7

    One look, one scene

    You take a scene from the course library, shot on set for Night Shift, and build a look that holds across every angle. A guest director sits in as the client.

    1. 7.1Building a look from references
    2. 7.2Shared nodes and groups
    3. 7.3A director in the room
    4. 7.4Taking notes without losing the grade
    The shot
    A library scene of your choice
    Hand in
    A look reference and the first pass of your scene

  • // 08 Deliver// 08 DeliverWeek 8

    Finish and hand over

    The last week is the last week of every job: final notes, quality control and files a distributor will accept. The cohort screens every scene on Thursday.

    1. 8.1Final notes and trims
    2. 8.2QC: legal levels, flicker, dropped frames
    3. 8.3Deliverables: master, web, archive
    4. 8.4The screening
    The shot
    Your library scene
    Hand in
    The finished scene delivered to spec, with grade notes

// 03 The shots// 03 The shots

The shots you grade

Three camera originals in log, graded in weeks 1 to 6. Drag the line to compare the flat file with the finished frame.

Night street

Weeks
1 to 4
Light
Sodium and LED
Hand in
Balanced and matched

Sodium street lamps, teal LEDs under the overpass and headlights, all in one frame. In log the street is one muddy grey.

You set black and white points on the waveform, pull the orange out of the shadows without killing the lamps, and let the overpass keep its colour.

Golden hour

Week
5
Light
Low sun, backlight
Hand in
Skin on the line

The sun sits behind her, so the face falls two stops under the field and the sky is close to clipping.

Gamma lifts the face, a soft window holds the sky, and a skin key puts her complexion on the vectorscope’s skin line instead of guessing by eye.

Mixed light

Week
6
Light
Tungsten and window
Hand in
Graded two ways

A caged bulb, green spill from a window and an old TV. Every source pulls the room a different way.

You separate the sources with windows and hue curves, decide that the lamp is the room’s light, and let the window stay cold behind him.
