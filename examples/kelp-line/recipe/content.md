## Content Direction

- **Tone:** welcoming, generous
- **Voice:** Speak directly to the visitor, like a host.
- **Headline style:** Invitations — "Come in", "Stay a while"
- **Headline examples:** "Every village deserves a library", "312 libraries built so far", "Where your gift goes"
- **Paragraph length:** 2–4 sentences (40–80 words); never more than 65 characters per line
- **CTA style:** A donate button with preset amounts, each showing what it achieves. Donate is the one primary action, with what each amount achieves; volunteering is the secondary one.
- **CTA examples:** "Donate", "Become a member", "Support our work", "Volunteer with us"
- **Content density:** Very low — one idea per screen, 40–80 words per section
- **Words to avoid:** "Elevate your brand", "The future of…", "Seamless experiences", "Unlock your potential", "Built for modern teams", "Cutting-edge", "Revolutionary", "World-class"

## Copy deck — write it before any layout

Write from the owner's own words — the name “Kelp Line” and “Volunteers replanting kelp forests on a cold northern coast: dives, beach days and a count of every plant in the water.” Every headline, line and claim grows from them: name what is really there — what is made, where, when, for whom. The headline and CTA examples are only the register, taken from another site: never reuse them. Anything you must invent (quotes, prices, names, numbers, dates) is marked in the copy deck as a placeholder for the owner to replace.

### Home
Establish mood and promise in seconds; orient the visitor to what this site is and give them a clear next step.

- **Hero:** Establish mood and promise immediately. One headline (≤ 8 words), one supporting line, one action.
- **Timeline:** Tell the story as dated steps. 4–7 real dates (years or months), each with what happened and one line on why it mattered.
- **Stats:** Prove it with a few real numbers. 3–4 true figures (years, projects, customers, ratings) with a label each.
- **Services:** Make offers clear. 4–6 services, each with a one-line description.
- **Editorial Story:** Tell the story behind the brand in a readable, magazine-like format. 150–300 words, one pull quote.
- **CTA Band:** Offer the next step mid-page without ending it. One specific offer (trial, deadline, free delivery) and its action.
- **Journal:** Show ongoing thinking and activity. Title, date, category.
- **Newsletter:** Turn a visit into a returning reader. What they get, how often, and that it is easy to leave.

### Our mission
Put a human face and point of view on the work; build trust.

- **About:** Put a human face and point of view on the work. Real names, real history, no mission-statement clichés.
- **Editorial Story:** Tell the story behind the brand in a readable, magazine-like format. 150–300 words, one pull quote.
- **Team:** Show the people behind it. 3–8 people: real photo, name, role, one sentence.
- **Stats:** Prove it with a few real numbers. 3–4 true figures (years, projects, customers, ratings) with a label each.

### Programs
Make the offer and the way of working clear enough to remove hesitation.

- **Services:** Make offers clear. 4–6 services, each with a one-line description.
- **Process:** Show what happens, step by step. Step name, 1–2 sentences, and how long it takes where that matters.
- **Stats:** Prove it with a few real numbers. 3–4 true figures (years, projects, customers, ratings) with a label each.
- **CTA Band:** Offer the next step mid-page without ending it. One specific offer (trial, deadline, free delivery) and its action.

### Stories
Let real clients or customers make the case, in their own words.

- **Testimonials:** Let real people vouch for the work. 3–4 real quotes, each with a name and who they are to this place (client, guest, customer, member).
- **Editorial Story:** Tell the story behind the brand in a readable, magazine-like format. 150–300 words, one pull quote.
- **Gallery:** Let atmosphere speak. 6–10 images with sparse captions.

### Donate
Turn a visitor into a donor: preset gifts that each say what they pay for, once or monthly, where the money goes, and a short form that hands off to payment.

- **Donate:** Turn support into a gift. 3–4 amounts with what each one buys, the split of spending in percent, one line on how payment happens.
- **Trust Strip:** Remove the small worries before a purchase or sign-up. The promises that matter for this kind of site (delivery, returns, guarantees, safety, what is included) — 2–5 words each plus one plain line.
- **FAQ:** Answer real objections. 5–8 genuine questions.

### Contact
Give one clear, low-friction way to get in touch.

- **Schedule:** Show what happens when. Every day of the programme, real times, short titles.
- **Closing CTA:** End with one clear next step. Headline in brand voice, one action, and the real ways to reach you (email, and phone or address where they exist).
- **Location:** Make visiting easy. Address, hours, transit notes.
