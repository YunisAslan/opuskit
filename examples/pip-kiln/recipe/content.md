## Content Direction

- **Tone:** cheeky, quick, confident
- **Voice:** Every heading is a small joke with a point: a problem, then its answer in the same breath. Short sentences. No jargon.
- **Headline style:** Problem? Answer. — “Hard story? Easily told.” “No talk. All pictures.”
- **Headline examples:** "Made to be used every day", "New this week", "Small batches, sent in two days"
- **Paragraph length:** 2–4 sentences (40–80 words); never more than 65 characters per line
- **CTA style:** Product pages, a cart and a short checkout path are prioritised. Clear "Add to bag" on product surfaces; editorial sections link to products, not to generic "Shop now".
- **CTA examples:** "Add to bag", "Shop the collection", "Buy now", "See them all"
- **Content density:** Low word count, high visual weight
- **Words to avoid:** "Elevate your brand", "The future of…", "Seamless experiences", "Unlock your potential", "Built for modern teams", "Cutting-edge", "Revolutionary", "World-class"

## Copy deck — write it before any layout

Write from the owner's own words — the name “Pip & Kiln” and “Bright glazed mugs, plates and vases from a two-person pottery, sold in our online shop. Saturday workshops at the wheel.” Every headline, line and claim grows from them: name what is really there — what is made, where, when, for whom. The headline and CTA examples are only the register, taken from another site: never reuse them. Anything you must invent (quotes, prices, names, numbers, dates) is marked in the copy deck as a placeholder for the owner to replace.

### Home
Establish mood and promise in seconds; orient the visitor to what this site is and give them a clear next step.

- **Hero:** Establish mood and promise immediately. One headline (≤ 8 words), one supporting line, one action.
- **Categories:** Show the range and let people go straight to their part of it. 3–8 categories, each with a real picture and an honest count.
- **Product Grid:** Browse and choose. Name, price, image, availability.
- **Collection:** Introduce the season or range. Collection name, one paragraph, season.
- **Manifesto:** State the point of view boldly. 1–3 sentences that could only be yours.
- **Testimonials:** Let real people vouch for the work. 3–4 real quotes, each with a name and who they are to this place (client, guest, customer, member).
- **Trust Strip:** Remove the small worries before a purchase or sign-up. The promises that matter for this kind of site (delivery, returns, guarantees, safety, what is included) — 2–5 words each plus one plain line.
- **Newsletter:** Turn a visit into a returning reader. What they get, how often, and that it is easy to leave.

### Shop
Let visitors browse and choose confidently.

- **Product Grid:** Browse and choose. Name, price, image, availability.
- **Product Highlight:** Show one product (or feature) in depth. What it is made of, its size or measure, and the one thing it does best.
- **Collection:** Introduce the season or range. Collection name, one paragraph, season.
- **FAQ:** Answer real objections. 5–8 genuine questions.

### Product
Show one product in full: gallery, price, variants, add-to-cart, and related items.

- **Product buy box:** Let people buy one product on its own page. 3–6 photos of the real product, one price, one option set, 3–4 short details.
- **Product Highlight:** Show one product (or feature) in depth. What it is made of, its size or measure, and the one thing it does best.
- **Testimonials:** Let real people vouch for the work. 3–4 real quotes, each with a name and who they are to this place (client, guest, customer, member).
- **FAQ:** Answer real objections. 5–8 genuine questions.
- **Product Grid:** Browse and choose. Name, price, image, availability.

### Cart
Let visitors review and adjust their selection before checkout.

- **The bag:** Each item with its picture, name, option and price; quantity to change, a way to remove it; the subtotal, what delivery costs or when it is free, and one button to checkout. Empty: one line and a way back to the shop.
- **Product Grid:** Browse and choose. Name, price, image, availability.

### Checkout
Collect payment and shipping with as little friction as possible.

- **Checkout:** Contact, delivery address, delivery choice, then payment — one column, the order summary beside it (above it on phones). Without a payment service, the last step says plainly that payment is not open yet and how to order by email instead.

### Workshops
Make the offer and the way of working clear enough to remove hesitation.

- **Services:** Make offers clear. 4–6 services, each with a one-line description.
- **Schedule:** Show what happens when. Every day of the programme, real times, short titles.
- **Gallery:** Let atmosphere speak. 6–10 images with sparse captions.
- **Pricing:** Remove the final uncertainty. Plan name, price, what's included.
- **Reservation:** Convert interest into a booking. Opening hours, group policy, phone number.
- **FAQ:** Answer real objections. 5–8 genuine questions.
