# Inkwell & Moth — Scrapbook Portfolio

Inkwell & Moth: The picture-book studio of illustrator Nell Arden: ink, watercolour and small night creatures, drawn by hand in an old bakery in Sheki. A portfolio with a scrapbook direction: cut and paste typography, a legal pad palette, illustration leading the experience and dynamic motion. Primary goal: get in touch.

Complexity: moderate · Recipe id: art-direction-studio

## The Big Idea — A playful way in

Visitors play to open the site; every item brings its own colours. Chosen by the owner.

Build the whole site around this one idea. Every page gets one moment that serves it, and nothing else competes with that moment.

- **What guides the scroll:** Play is the guide: the site invites a small action first, and keeps answering the visitor’s hand — things react, colours change, nothing is only to be read.
- **How each chapter opens:** Chapters change colour: each item or part brings its own ground and ink as it arrives.
- **The moment people remember:** The gate: one playful action (drag, hold, pick) that opens the site — three seconds, always skippable.
- **How the site ends:** A footer worth reaching: the brand name assembles letter by letter, as a small reward for scrolling to the end.

It reaches the pages through these signature moments (details in the motion recipe): A playful way in — Home — Hero — Illustrated hero; Each item brings its own colours — Home — Featured Work.

## Creative Direction

**Mood:** Personal, Warm, Handmade

**Personality:** Taped photos, torn paper, handwritten notes

### Visual principles
- Collected, not designed
- Every photo has a caption
- Imperfect edges, tidy reading

### Do
- Rotate photos ±2–4° with a tape strip or shadow
- Marker titles, typewriter captions and dates
- A paper texture on the ground, plain text on top

### Avoid
- Collage behind reading text
- Too many fonts
- Perfectly aligned grids

### Not the generic AI look
- A cream or beige page ground with a clay/terracotta accent
- A near-black ground with one acid-green or orange accent, or tinted charcoal standing in for black
- Small uppercase, letter-spaced monospace labels above every heading
- Numbered markers (01 / 02) on content that is not a real sequence
- Meta strings joined with middle dots or spaced em dashes, and "→" appended to links
- One italic or coloured accent word inside an otherwise plain headline
- Falling back to Inter, Space Grotesk, Syne or Fraunces instead of the recipe's fonts

### Design principles
- Collected, not designed
- Every photo has a caption
- Imperfect edges, tidy reading
- Scroll-linked parallax, line-by-line type reveals, media transitions between sections.

## Award checklist

What separates an award-winning site from a good template (from a study of 12 Awwwards sites). Check every page against it.

- One idea: everything serves “A playful way in”. A part that doesn’t serve it gets quieter, not louder.
- One unforgettable moment per page — and only one. Everything else on that page supports it.
- Type scale contrast: the biggest Permanent Marker size is at least 6× the body size on desktop, labels stay small (11–14 px, Courier Prime), and nothing in between competes.
- Motion choreography: one thing moves at a time; each arrival enters, holds and leaves; staggers of 40–80 ms; the same one or two easings everywhere.
- The first seconds: the first screen is complete and readable before anything animates.
- Mobile is its own composition: headlines re-broken by hand, media re-cropped, pinned and hover effects replaced by their mobile versions — never a squeezed desktop.
- The ending is designed: the footer (One quiet line) is a moment — a footer worth reaching: the brand name assembles letter by letter, as a small reward for scrolling to the end.
- Craft details: text selection in the accent colour, a favicon from the logo, designed focus states, no layout shift, real copy everywhere, a 404 page in the same voice.
- Smooth is part of the effect: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop; animate transform and opacity only; nothing runs off-screen.

## Why It Works

### Why the visual direction works
Feels personal and honest — strong for weddings, family businesses, travel, makers and anyone whose story is in their photos.

### Why the typography works
A marker headline like a label on a box, and typewriter text like a note taped next to a photo.

### Why the palette works
Taken from an object everyone knows — yellow paper, blue pen, a red margin — so it feels specific and human rather than a "tasteful neutral".

### Why the layout works
Breaking expectations makes a site memorable — as long as one clear anchor per section keeps it usable.

### Why the motion works
Scroll-linked motion makes the visitor feel in control of the story. Here, fade & rise reveal and image clip reveal serve the story: give sections a calm entrance so content arrives in reading order.

### Why the chosen assets work
Illustration makes a brand unmistakably ownable — nobody else has the same drawings.
