## Content Direction

- **Tone:** assured, cultured
- **Voice:** Confident understatement; assume an informed reader.
- **Headline style:** Understated, literary
- **Headline examples:** "14 June, Sheki", "Join us", "The day, hour by hour"
- **Paragraph length:** 2–4 sentences (40–80 words); never more than 65 characters per line
- **CTA style:** Address, opening hours and a map sit in the hero and the footer of every page. One RSVP button with the date beside it, repeated after the schedule.
- **CTA examples:** "Get directions", "See opening hours", "Plan your visit", "RSVP"
- **Content density:** Medium — varies intentionally by section
- **Words to avoid:** "Elevate your brand", "The future of…", "Seamless experiences", "Unlock your potential", "Built for modern teams", "Cutting-edge", "Revolutionary", "World-class"

## Copy deck — write it before any layout

Write from the owner's own words — the name “Pale Hour” and “A photography gallery and bookshop in an old print works: three exhibitions a year, talks on Thursdays, photobooks to take home.” Every headline, line and claim grows from them: name what is really there — what is made, where, when, for whom. The headline and CTA examples are only the register, taken from another site: never reuse them. Anything you must invent (quotes, prices, names, numbers, dates) is marked in the copy deck as a placeholder for the owner to replace.

### Home
Establish mood and promise in seconds; orient the visitor to what this site is and give them a clear next step.

- **Hero:** Establish mood and promise immediately. One headline (≤ 8 words), one supporting line, one action.
- **Intro:** Say what this is and who it is for. 1–2 sentences, specific and concrete.
- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.
- **Schedule:** Show what happens when. Every day of the programme, real times, short titles.
- **Journal:** Show ongoing thinking and activity. Title, date, category.

### Exhibitions
Prove quality and range through the strongest projects; make it easy to go deeper on any one.

- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.
- **Gallery:** Let atmosphere speak. 6–10 images with sparse captions.

### Visit
Help visitors find and choose the right location.

- **Location:** Make visiting easy. Address, hours, transit notes.
- **Schedule:** Show what happens when. Every day of the programme, real times, short titles.
- **FAQ:** Answer real objections. 5–8 genuine questions.

### About
Put a human face and point of view on the work; build trust.

- **About:** Put a human face and point of view on the work. Real names, real history, no mission-statement clichés.
- **Team:** Show the people behind it. 3–8 people: real photo, name, role, one sentence.
