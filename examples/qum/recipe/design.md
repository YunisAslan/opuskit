# QUM — Warm Scandinavian Minimal Store

QUM: Skincare made in small batches on the Absheron coast, with Caspian salt and saffron grown a few kilometres inland. Six products, one ritual. A store with a scandinavian minimal direction: soft wedge typography, a sage white palette, photography leading the experience and subtle motion. Primary goal: buy something.

Complexity: light · Recipe id: organic-modern

## The Big Idea — A walk through named stops

Scrolling moves visitors from place to place, each with a small card. Chosen by the owner.

Build the whole site around this one idea. Every page gets one moment that serves it, and nothing else competes with that moment.

- **What guides the scroll:** The site is a place to walk through: every main view is a named stop (The terrace, Room 4, Day two), and a quiet index shows where the visitor is.
- **How each chapter opens:** Each chapter is a stop: a full-screen view first, then a small card that names it and says one useful thing.
- **The moment people remember:** The walk itself — the stops section, where scrolling feels like moving through the real place.
- **How the site ends:** The last stop is the way in: how to book, visit or arrive, with a line that is true right now (open, rooms left, doors at 19:00).

It reaches the pages through these signature moments (details in the motion recipe): A walk through named stops — The salt — Location; A live status line — Navigation.

## Creative Direction

**Mood:** Light, Friendly, Functional, Warm

**Personality:** Warm — welcoming and human

### Visual principles
- Function first, softness second
- Light backgrounds, tactile details
- Clear, generous hierarchy

### Do
- Use daylight photography
- Keep corners softly rounded (4–8px) where needed
- Write short, plain sentences

### Avoid
- Pure white + pure black
- Dramatic dark sections
- Ornamental type

### Not the generic AI look
- A cream or beige page ground with a clay/terracotta accent
- A near-black ground with one acid-green or orange accent, or tinted charcoal standing in for black
- Small uppercase, letter-spaced monospace labels above every heading
- Numbered markers (01 / 02) on content that is not a real sequence
- Meta strings joined with middle dots or spaced em dashes, and "→" appended to links
- One italic or coloured accent word inside an otherwise plain headline
- Falling back to Inter, Space Grotesk, Syne or Fraunces instead of the recipe's fonts

### Design principles
- Function first, softness second
- Light backgrounds, tactile details
- Clear, generous hierarchy
- Content eases in once as it enters the viewport; media drifts slightly. Nothing loops.

## Award checklist

What separates an award-winning site from a good template (from a study of 12 Awwwards sites). Check every page against it.

- One idea: everything serves “A walk through named stops”. A part that doesn’t serve it gets quieter, not louder.
- One unforgettable moment per page — and only one. Everything else on that page supports it.
- Type scale contrast: the biggest Petrona size is at least 6× the body size on desktop, labels stay small (11–14 px, Public Sans), and nothing in between competes.
- Motion choreography: one thing moves at a time; each arrival enters, holds and leaves; staggers of 40–80 ms; the same one or two easings everywhere.
- The first seconds: the first screen is complete and readable before anything animates.
- Mobile is its own composition: headlines re-broken by hand, media re-cropped, pinned and hover effects replaced by their mobile versions — never a squeezed desktop.
- The ending is designed: the footer (Everything, listed) is a moment — the last stop is the way in: how to book, visit or arrive, with a line that is true right now (open, rooms left, doors at 19:00).
- Craft details: text selection in the accent colour, a favicon from the logo, designed focus states, no layout shift, real copy everywhere, a 404 page in the same voice.
- Smooth is part of the effect: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop; animate transform and opacity only; nothing runs off-screen.

## Why It Works

### Why the visual direction works
Soft light tones and humanist type feel approachable while the disciplined structure keeps everything easy to use.

### Why the typography works
Reckless Neue, Signifier and Teodor are the serifs of 2026 award sites (Colonia Zacamil, Mosby Files, Partizan): low-contrast, light, a little soft at the joins. Petrona (100–900 + italics) is the closest free family in that register; Public Sans keeps the interface plain.

### Why the palette works
A barely-green white (Tengile, United in Football) that warms photography of people and land without turning into a green theme.

### Why the layout works
Symmetry lowers cognitive load — visitors instantly know where to look and how to move through the page.

### Why the motion works
Subtle reveals give rhythm and polish without distracting from reading. Here, soft fade and image clip reveal serve the story: let sections settle in like light changing — nothing moves, it simply appears.

### Why the chosen assets work
Consistent photography builds atmosphere faster than any UI element — it is the most direct way to make visitors feel something.
