# Brasshand — Typography First Agency Site

Brasshand: A small branding agency in Baku — names, identities and campaigns for food, music and culture. An agency site with a typography first direction: poster caps typography, a lido blue palette, typography leading the experience and dynamic motion. Primary goal: get in touch.

Complexity: moderate · Recipe id: typography-first

## The Big Idea — Chapters in giant words

Each part opens on one huge word; proof arrives one item at a time. Chosen by the owner.

Build the whole site around this one idea. Every page gets one moment that serves it, and nothing else competes with that moment.

- **What guides the scroll:** Type is the guide: one display size far above everything else, used only for the chapter words, so the page has a rhythm of loud word → quiet content → loud word.
- **How each chapter opens:** Every main chapter opens with one word set huge and cropped at the screen edges — the chapter’s subject (Work, Menu, Programme), never a slogan.
- **The moment people remember:** The proof chapter: it holds still while its projects, figures or quotes replace each other one at a time, each owning the screen for a moment.
- **How the site ends:** The brand name set as the last giant word, across the full width of the footer.

It reaches the pages through these signature moments (details in the motion recipe): Chapters that open with a giant word — Home — Manifesto; Proof, one at a time — Home — Featured Work.

## Creative Direction

**Mood:** Bold, Articulate, Graphic

**Personality:** A writer with strong opinions and a sense of humour.

### Visual principles
- The words are the image
- Scale contrast of at least 8:1 between display and body
- Every line break is designed by hand
- Media is punctuation, not wallpaper

### Do
- Set hero words at 12–20vw
- Use a variable display font and animate one axis on scroll
- Break lines manually per breakpoint
- Keep the whole page on the blue — white type, one pink mark

### Avoid
- Filling space with stock photos
- More than two families
- Auto-wrapped display text
- Low contrast "aesthetic" greys

### Not the generic AI look
- A cream or beige page ground with a clay/terracotta accent
- A near-black ground with one acid-green or orange accent, or tinted charcoal standing in for black
- Small uppercase, letter-spaced monospace labels above every heading
- Numbered markers (01 / 02) on content that is not a real sequence
- Meta strings joined with middle dots or spaced em dashes, and "→" appended to links
- One italic or coloured accent word inside an otherwise plain headline
- Falling back to Inter, Space Grotesk, Syne or Fraunces instead of the recipe's fonts

## Award checklist

What separates an award-winning site from a good template (from a study of 12 Awwwards sites). Check every page against it.

- One idea: everything serves “Chapters in giant words”. A part that doesn’t serve it gets quieter, not louder.
- One unforgettable moment per page — and only one. Everything else on that page supports it.
- Type scale contrast: the biggest Bayon size is at least 6× the body size on desktop, labels stay small (11–14 px, Reddit Sans), and nothing in between competes.
- Motion choreography: one thing moves at a time; each arrival enters, holds and leaves; staggers of 40–80 ms; the same one or two easings everywhere.
- The first seconds: the preloader follows real loading and is gone within 2.5 s; the first screen is complete the moment it lifts.
- Mobile is its own composition: headlines re-broken by hand, media re-cropped, pinned and hover effects replaced by their mobile versions — never a squeezed desktop.
- The ending is designed: the footer (Big name) is a moment — the brand name set as the last giant word, across the full width of the footer.
- Craft details: text selection in the accent colour, a favicon from the logo, designed focus states, no layout shift, real copy everywhere, a 404 page in the same voice.
- Smooth is part of the effect: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop; animate transform and opacity only; nothing runs off-screen.

## Why It Works

### Why the visual direction works
When type carries the design, the message and the visual become one. It is fast, accessible, and impossible to confuse with a template.

### Why the typography works
Tall, tightly-set capitals shout like a flyer while a plain grotesk does the talking (Dontboardme pairs Bayon with Neue Montreal). Fresher than Bebas or Anton, which every template uses.

### Why the palette works
A saturated mid blue as the whole page is sunny and editorial at once (Inside the Head uses it as a chapter field). Burgundy is the unexpected, grown-up accent.

### Why the layout works
Editorial grids pair narrow reading columns with generous media, mimicking print — content feels curated and worth reading.

### Why the motion works
Scroll-linked motion makes the visitor feel in control of the story. Here, fade & rise reveal and line-by-line headline reveal serve the story: give sections a calm entrance so content arrives in reading order.

### Why the chosen assets work
Typography-led sites are fast, accessible and distinctive — the words and the visual become the same thing.
