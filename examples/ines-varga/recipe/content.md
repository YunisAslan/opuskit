## Content Direction

- **Tone:** direct, blunt
- **Voice:** Plain words. Lowercase is fine. No polish for polish's sake.
- **Headline style:** Blunt, loud, short
- **Headline examples:** "Selected work, 2019—2026", "Design for things that last", "Currently taking on new projects"
- **Paragraph length:** 2–4 sentences (40–80 words); never more than 65 characters per line
- **CTA style:** Getting in touch is one step away on every page — the menu’s action and the footer’s email, plus the closing part on the pages that have one; a real email address is always visible. One quiet, persistent contact link plus a direct email at the end of every case study.
- **CTA examples:** "Start a project", "Say hello", "Email us", "See the work"
- **Content density:** High but structured — modules keep it scannable
- **Words to avoid:** "Elevate your brand", "The future of…", "Seamless experiences", "Unlock your potential", "Built for modern teams", "Cutting-edge", "Revolutionary", "World-class", "Empower", "Supercharge", "Unleash", "Harness the power", "Best-in-class", "Industry-leading", "Next-generation", "Game-changer", "Future-proof", "Trusted by leading…"

## Copy deck — write it before any layout

Write from the owner's own words — the name “Ines Varga” and “Illustrated maps for towns, trails and books, drawn by hand in ink and watercolour. Commissions open.” Every headline, line and claim grows from them: name what is really there — what is made, where, when, for whom. The headline and CTA examples are only the register, taken from another site: never reuse them. Anything you must invent (quotes, prices, names, numbers, dates) is marked in the copy deck as a placeholder for the owner to replace — and is invented outright: never a real film, book, record, artwork, artist, brand or famous person standing in for the owner's own (Ninth Row, #25).

### Home
Establish mood and promise in seconds; orient the visitor to what this site is and give them a clear next step.

- **Hero:** Establish mood and promise immediately. One headline (≤ 8 words), one supporting line, one action.
- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.
- **Closing CTA:** End with one clear next step. Headline in brand voice, one action, and the real ways to reach you (email, and phone or address where they exist).

### Selected Work
Prove quality and range through the strongest projects; make it easy to go deeper on any one.

- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.
- **Clients:** Show breadth of trust. Real client names only.

### Project
Show one project in full: what it was, the facts (client, place, year, role), the pictures in order, and the way to the next project.

- **Case Study Preview:** Show depth on one project. Problem, approach, result — 3 short paragraphs.
- **Gallery:** Let atmosphere speak. 6–10 images with sparse captions.
- **Specs:** Give the facts people check before deciding. 6–12 real facts — dimensions, materials, rooms and area, year, place, what is included.
- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.

### About
Put a human face and point of view on the work; build trust.

- **About:** Put a human face and point of view on the work. Real names, real history, no mission-statement clichés.
- **Testimonials:** Let real people vouch for the work. 3–4 real quotes, each with a name and who they are to this place (client, guest, customer, member).
- **Timeline:** Tell the story as dated steps. 4–7 real dates (years or months), each with what happened and one line on why it mattered.

### Contact
Give one clear, low-friction way to get in touch.

- **Closing CTA:** End with one clear next step. Headline in brand voice, one action, and the real ways to reach you (email, and phone or address where they exist).
- **FAQ:** Answer real objections. 5–8 genuine questions.
