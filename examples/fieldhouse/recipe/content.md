## Content Direction

- **Tone:** composed, precise
- **Voice:** Measured sentences, no exclamation marks.
- **Headline style:** Short, poised statements
- **Headline examples:** "A small studio with a point of view", "Made slowly, on purpose", "Recent work"
- **Paragraph length:** 2–4 sentences (40–80 words); never more than 65 characters per line
- **CTA style:** Every page ends with a contact prompt; a real email address is always visible. Low-pressure: "Say hello" or "Visit the studio", placed after the work has spoken.
- **CTA examples:** "Start a project", "Say hello", "Email us", "Visit the studio"
- **Content density:** Very low — images dominate, copy is caption-length
- **Words to avoid:** "Elevate your brand", "The future of…", "Seamless experiences", "Unlock your potential", "Built for modern teams", "Cutting-edge", "Revolutionary", "World-class"

## Copy deck — write it before any layout

Write from the owner's own words — the name “Fieldhouse” and “An architecture studio that turns old barns into houses — keeping the timber, the stone and the light.” Every headline, line and claim grows from them: name what is really there — what is made, where, when, for whom. The headline and CTA examples are only the register, taken from another site: never reuse them. Anything you must invent (quotes, prices, names, numbers, dates) is marked in the copy deck as a placeholder for the owner to replace.

### Home
Establish mood and promise in seconds; orient the visitor to what this site is and give them a clear next step.

- **Hero:** Establish mood and promise immediately. One headline (≤ 8 words), one supporting line, one action.
- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.
- **Manifesto:** State the point of view boldly. 1–3 sentences that could only be yours.
- **Testimonials:** Let real people vouch for the work. 3–4 real quotes, each with a name and role.
- **Closing CTA:** End with one clear next step. Headline in brand voice, email address.

### Work
Prove quality and range through the strongest projects; make it easy to go deeper on any one.

- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.
- **Gallery:** Let atmosphere speak. 6–10 images with sparse captions.

### Project
Show one project in full: what it was, the facts (client, place, year, role), the pictures in order, and the way to the next project.

- **Case Study Preview:** Show depth on one project. Problem, approach, result — 3 short paragraphs.
- **Gallery:** Let atmosphere speak. 6–10 images with sparse captions.
- **Specs:** Give the facts people check before deciding. 6–12 real facts — dimensions, materials, rooms and area, year, place, what is included.
- **Featured Work:** Prove quality with 3–6 best projects. Project title, discipline, year, 1 image each.

### About
Put a human face and point of view on the work; build trust.

- **About:** Put a human face and point of view on the work. Real names, real history, no mission-statement clichés.
- **Process:** Reduce uncertainty about working together. Step name, 1–2 sentences, duration.
- **Team:** Show the people behind it. 3–8 people: real photo, name, role, one sentence.

### Contact
Give one clear, low-friction way to get in touch.

- **Closing CTA:** End with one clear next step. Headline in brand voice, email address.
- **Location:** Make visiting easy. Address, hours, transit notes.
