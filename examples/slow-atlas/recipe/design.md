# Slow Atlas — News Grid Magazine

Slow Atlas: An independent magazine of long-form travel essays — one place, told slowly. A magazine with a news grid direction: newsroom typography, a cherry red palette, typography leading the experience and subtle motion. Primary goal: subscribe.

Complexity: light · Recipe id: swiss-modern

## The Big Idea — Loud covers, quiet reading

Covers are loud and giant; the reading underneath stays calm. It suits a magazine with a News Grid look at subtle movement.

Build the whole site around this one idea. Every page gets one moment that serves it, and nothing else competes with that moment.

- **What guides the scroll:** Two volumes, never mixed: covers (the first screen, chapter openers, the start of each story) are loud — giant type, a full-bleed photo — and everything people read stays narrow, calm and quiet.
- **How each chapter opens:** Chapters open like magazine covers: one giant word or a full-width photo, then the calm column starts.
- **The moment people remember:** The start of a story: a cover that opens like a curtain onto the reading column.
- **How the site ends:** A quiet ending: the footer is as calm as the reading, with the name set large once.

It reaches the pages through these signature moments (details in the motion recipe): Chapters that open with a giant word — Home — Journal; Photos revealed like a curtain — Home — Editorial Story.

## Creative Direction

**Mood:** Direct, Informed, Urgent

**Personality:** Bold headlines, newspaper structure

### Visual principles
- Headlines do the work
- Columns create order
- One strong colour for emphasis

### Do
- Use big, bold headlines
- Organise content in clear columns
- Use rules to separate stories

### Avoid
- Decorative images
- Soft pastels
- Centered paragraphs

### Not the generic AI look
- A cream or beige page ground with a clay/terracotta accent
- A near-black ground with one acid-green or orange accent, or tinted charcoal standing in for black
- Small uppercase, letter-spaced monospace labels above every heading
- Numbered markers (01 / 02) on content that is not a real sequence
- Meta strings joined with middle dots or spaced em dashes, and "→" appended to links
- One italic or coloured accent word inside an otherwise plain headline
- Falling back to Inter, Space Grotesk, Syne or Fraunces instead of the recipe's fonts

### Design principles
- Headlines do the work
- Columns create order
- One strong colour for emphasis
- Content eases in once as it enters the viewport; media drifts slightly. Nothing loops.

## Award checklist

What separates an award-winning site from a good template (from a study of 12 Awwwards sites). Check every page against it.

- One idea: everything serves “Loud covers, quiet reading”. A part that doesn’t serve it gets quieter, not louder.
- One unforgettable moment per page — and only one. Everything else on that page supports it.
- Type scale contrast: the biggest Schibsted Grotesk size is at least 6× the body size on desktop, labels stay small (11–14 px, Schibsted Grotesk), and nothing in between competes.
- Motion choreography: one thing moves at a time; each arrival enters, holds and leaves; staggers of 40–80 ms; the same one or two easings everywhere.
- The first seconds: the preloader follows real loading and is gone within 2.5 s; the first screen is complete the moment it lifts.
- Mobile is its own composition: headlines re-broken by hand, media re-cropped, pinned and hover effects replaced by their mobile versions — never a squeezed desktop.
- The ending is designed: the footer (Signature columns) is a moment — a quiet ending: the footer is as calm as the reading, with the name set large once.
- Craft details: text selection in the accent colour, a favicon from the logo, designed focus states, no layout shift, real copy everywhere, a 404 page in the same voice.
- Smooth is part of the effect: 60 fps on a mid-range laptop; animate transform and opacity only; nothing runs off-screen.

## Why It Works

### Why the visual direction works
Newspaper structure is instantly understood — visitors scan headlines and find what matters fast.

### Why the typography works
Drawn for a Scandinavian newspaper group: punchy grotesk headlines over a serif made for long reading — the modern newsroom look.

### Why the palette works
Cherry red is the fashion colour of the moment: loud, glossy and instantly recognisable. As a full ground it feels like a campaign, not a website.

### Why the layout works
A visible modular grid makes dense content scannable and gives the brand an objective, confident structure.

### Why the motion works
Subtle reveals give rhythm and polish without distracting from reading. Here, fade & rise reveal and line-by-line headline reveal serve the story: give sections a calm entrance so content arrives in reading order.

### Why the chosen assets work
Typography-led sites are fast, accessible and distinctive — the words and the visual become the same thing.
