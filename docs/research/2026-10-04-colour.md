# Colour research: why Awwwards palettes never feel off-putting, and how to fix ours (2026-10-04)

**Question from the owner:** some of our palettes look old/dated and off-putting, while sites on Awwwards use many different colours and none of them feel that way. Why, and what should change?

**Method.** 144 live sites from Awwwards: Sites of the Day/Month, Honors, 23 category listings (architecture, fashion, food & drink, hotel & restaurant, real estate, technology, music, culture & education, events, institutions, e-commerce, agencies, portfolio, startups, health, games, photography, corporate, magazines, luxury, sports, experimental, illustration) and 9 collections (Color Exploration, Pastel Colors, Black & White, NonProfit, Photography, Brutalism, Trippy, Storytelling). Five were dropped because the domain is now spam or parked, which leaves **n = 139**. For each site, headed Chrome at 1440×900 took a first-screen and a mid-page screenshot. From the DOM: computed backgrounds weighted by area, text colours weighted by characters × font size, link and button colours, and the share of the first screen covered by media. From the screenshots: a 10-colour median-cut palette, and the share of pixels with OKLCH chroma > 0.08 ("vivid") and > 0.15 ("strong"). Everything was converted to OKLab/OKLCH with the same maths as `src/lib/color.ts`. Raw data: `docs/research/2026-10-04-colour-sites.json`. Scripts and screenshots are in the session scratchpad (`colour/`).

Then each of our 42 palettes was rendered as a mini page (nav, headline, body, button, card, accent link). The built examples were reviewed too (aster-house, hane, halvik, brasshand, …). Every proposal was checked with a script that imports `src/lib/color.ts` and repeats the palette rules from `scripts/check.ts` exactly.

---

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Award sites do have many colours, but **almost none of that colour is in the page ground**. 85% of the 139 sites sit on a neutral ground (OKLCH chroma < 0.025): white or barely-warm white (44%), or black/near-black (42%). Their colour comes from **photos, film and 3D** (23% of sites) or from **one saturated accent covering ~0–8% of the UI** (33%). When a site does put colour in the ground (≈ 12%), it **commits fully**: Klein blue `#0000EE`, signal red `#DE2B2B`, violet `#660099`, poster yellow `#FFCE00`, leaf green `#8ED462`. Or it goes very pale (`#FFEDD7`, `#CBF2D9`), or very deep (`#42041E`, `#171E39`). **Only 5 of 139 grounds (3.6%) sit in the "dusty middle"**: mid lightness (L 0.36–0.89) with mid chroma (0.02–0.12), the greyed pastels and faded mid-tones. **11 of our 42 palettes (26%) sit there, and 6 more lean on it.** That band is what reads as dated: greyed colour looks like faded print, 1990s/2000s UI themes (Win95 teal, powder-blue Geocities, mauve, pea-soup, beige office) or a sun-bleached "millennial" palette. Two habits make it worse. We tint text toward a different hue (navy on pink, maroon on blue, forest green on pink); 80% of award sites use neutral text (C < 0.02). And our secondaries/surfaces are a darker shade of the same mid colour, which turns into tone-on-tone mid-colour blocks.

**The root cause is structural.** `check.ts` requires every ground to be ≥ 0.06 ΔE_OK from every other ground. That rule pushes a library outwards across colour space. You can fit only ~3 neutral light grounds (white, warm paper, cool grey) and ~5 dark ones before they collide, so every new palette was forced into a *coloured* ground, and the free room was the dusty middle. Award sites differ through **accent, type, media and layout**, and very little through the ground. Our rule measures only the ground.

### 2.1 Grounds
- **Light neutrals:** L median 0.995 (p10 0.925). 28 of 61 are pure `#FFFFFF`. The other 18 tinted ones are **warm** off-whites (H 60–110, C 0.006–0.023: `#F1EEE9` Noho, `#F2EFEA` Floema, `#EBE6E0` Colonia Zacamil, `#EFEAE0` United in Football). There are **no cool-tinted light grounds**. Cool comes from a plain grey instead (`#E0DEE1` Mille Dollars, `#F3F3F3` Sharplink).
- **Dark neutrals:** L median 0.15. 18 of 58 are pure `#000000`; the rest are near-black `#111`–`#2C2824`. Cool tints (7) are more common than warm (3), e.g. `#181D21` Boc Studio, `#08090D` State of the Gallery, `#2C2824` Son Daven, `#27201D` LXL Creative.
- **Committed colour grounds** are always a *full* colour (C 0.14–0.30) with pure white or pure black type: `#990000` Meer Mohsin, `#660099` Catapultismo, `#32006F` Nodenza, `#0000EE` Love Letters from Craig, `#DE2B2B` Delice, `#FFCE00` House That Yauch Built / NY Philharmonic, `#8ED462` MindMarket, `#009DD8` Hans Sleutelaar archive.
- **Tinted grounds are either pale or deep**: pale `#FFEDD7` Oryzo, `#F5E4C7` Buck's Sauce, `#CBF2D9` Perform Europe; deep `#171E39` Trevor Noah, `#42041E` Schlosshotel Kitzbühel, `#37482F` Masia Mont Rural.

### 2.2 Accents
- One accent, saturated (median C 0.138; 44% ≥ 0.15), used on **links, one button, one band or one tag**. Typical UI coverage is under 1% of the area, 8% at p75. A third of sites have no accent at all and let photography carry everything.
- Common accent hues are evenly spread: red-orange `#FF4421`/`#EE421B`/`#D82F2F`, pure blue `#0000EE`/`#0011FF`/`#0E76FF`, violet `#8051FF`/`#4F46E5`, green `#2BA662`/`#00FB96`, yellow `#FFE500`/`#ECFF3E`. **No accent family is "safe" or "dated" in itself. Area and chroma decide.**
- Pastel accents are used **only on dark grounds** (`#FF9BB4` on Trevor Noah navy, `#ACFFCE` on Byotone, `#CDABFE` on AI in Design). There they read as light, not muddy.

### 2.3 Text
Neutral. 80% have chroma < 0.02, and 42% are pure black or white. When text is tinted, it is tinted **toward the ground's own hue** (Delice's cream `#F8EFDC` on red, Mont Rural's parchment `#F8E8C5` on moss), never to a contrasting hue.

### 2.4 Media, gradients, grain
Of the 15 most colourful first screens, 8 get their colour from **photos, film or 3D** (Dilla Dimension, Neonika, Humbleteam, PP Neue Montreal, Hobro Digital, Jacob How, NY Philharmonic, Wonder Vision). The other 7 get it from a **committed UI colour** (Love Letters `#0000EE`, Catapultismo `#660099`, MindMarket `#8ED462`, Delice `#DE2B2B`), plus the two dusty exceptions Sick Agency and Linus Bio. Gradients appear **inside media** (3D renders, blurred photos, light-leak video) and almost never as a UI wash. Grain can't be measured from 1440 px JPEGs; by eye it shows up on 3D/photo-led sites, not as a flat CSS overlay. Colour chapters appear as **one full band or one inverted section** (5% coloured band, 19% light↔dark inversion), not as rotating pastel sections.

### 2.5 Why nothing feels off on Awwwards
1. **Grounds are either neutral or decisive.** A grey-ish mid colour signals "faded"; a full colour or a clean white/black signals "chosen".
2. **One colour job at a time.** The accent is rare and loud, and media brings the rest. Two or more mid-chroma colours of similar weight next to each other (our ground + secondary + tinted text + accent) is what reads as an old theme.
3. **Ink is ink.** Neutral black/white text keeps any ground looking clean.
4. **Contrast is high** (median 15.7:1), and the steps between ground and surface are small and neutral (ΔL 0.02–0.04) or fully inverted (black block on white).
5. **The colour of the content leads.** Photos, product and film decide the temperature, and the UI stays out of the way.

## 5. Usage rules, looks, and why each never feels off

**Rules that apply to every palette** (put them in `colorRoles.usage` and the Build Package's visual-direction skill):
1. **Accent is a signal, not a colour scheme.** On award sites it covers 0.1% of the UI at the median and ~8% at p75. It goes on link hover/underline, *one* primary button or *one* key number per view, the focus ring, the active nav item, and at most one full-width band per page (a "chapter"). It never goes on body text, icon sets, borders or card fills.
2. **Ink is neutral.** Text and primary are near-black or near-white, tinted at most toward the ground's own hue (C ≤ 0.04). Never a contrasting hue (navy on pink, maroon on blue, green on pink).
3. **Surfaces step, they don't shade.** Surface/secondary sit ΔL 0.02–0.05 from the ground in the same hue, or invert fully (black block on white, white card on colour). Never a darker mid-tone of a coloured ground.
4. **A coloured ground is all or nothing.** Use a committed field (C ≥ 0.12) with pure black/white ink, a clear pale (L ≥ 0.86, C ≥ 0.05), or a deep one (L < 0.36). Nothing greyed in between.
5. **When media leads, the ground goes neutral.** With photography, video or 3D as the lead, the palette's job is to frame. The accent may be picked from the media (the complement of its dominant hue).
6. **One colour field per screen.** A committed ground plus photos plus the accent is the maximum. Chapters come one at a time as you scroll, never two coloured blocks side by side.

## 6. The rule change that unlocks the right direction

`check.ts` asks for 0.06 ΔE_OK between *grounds* only. That treats "white + cobalt" and "white + vermilion" as duplicates, while "greyed teal" and "greyed sage" pass as different. The sampled sites show the opposite: identity comes from **ground + accent** (plus type and media). A palette-level rule keeps the anti-cluster intent (cream band ≤ 1, clay ≤ 1 stay as they are) and stops pushing palettes into the dusty middle. Verified: Set A + Set B (42 palettes) passes this version; Set A alone passes the current one.

```ts
// Two palettes collide only when both their grounds and their accents are close.
for (let i = 0; i < pal.length; i++) for (let j = i + 1; j < pal.length; j++) {
  const a = pal[i], b = pal[j], d = deltaE(a.colors.background, b.colors.background)
  if (d >= 0.06) continue
  assert.ok(d >= 0.02 && a.dark === b.dark && deltaE(a.colors.accent, b.colors.accent) >= 0.12,
    `palettes too similar: ${a.id} ~ ${b.id} (ground ΔE ${d.toFixed(3)})`)
}
// The dusty middle: greyed mid-tone grounds read as dated (3.6% of 139 award sites, 26% of our old set).
const dusty = pal.filter((p) => { const o = oklab(p.colors.background)
  return o.L >= 0.36 && o.L < 0.89 && o.C >= 0.02 && o.C < 0.12 && !(o.L >= 0.86 && o.C >= 0.05) })
assert.deepEqual(dusty.map((p) => p.id), [], 'muddy mid-tone grounds — commit (C ≥ 0.12), go pale (L ≥ 0.86, C ≥ 0.05) or go deep (L < 0.36)')
```

Two exceptions to know about: `arcade` (.354) and `chrome-silver` (C .013) sit just outside the dusty test on purpose. They are genre palettes, kept to pixel-art and Y2K/sketch respectively. A softer, review-only signal: text chroma above 0.05 (today legal-pad's ballpoint navy, lido-blue, bubblegum; all deliberate).

With the relaxed rule, `wet-concrete` can keep its original lighter ground `#E1E5EA` (also verified) instead of stepping down to `#C6CCD3`.

## 7. Choosing a palette by fit, not by "unused"

Today the kit takes `plan.palette ?? direction.defaults.palette`, and the earlier fallback picked whatever was unused. The sampled sites suggest a fit recipe in four steps. It needs no hand tagging, because each palette's attributes can be computed from its hex values (zone, temperature, accent hue).

**Step 1: where does colour live?** (from `lead`, the uploaded media, motion)
- `lead` ∈ photography / video / 3d, or real photos uploaded → **neutral ground** (light or dark neutral, or limestone/sage-white/warm-black). Colour lives in the media (23% of award sites; most luxury, hotel, real-estate, architecture, fashion).
- `lead` = typography / illustration, no photos → **colour can live in the UI**: a committed ground (klein-field, grape-soda, hazard-yellow, yerba-leaf, signal-orange, …) or a clear pale.
- `product` → neutral or a single committed product colour (console-lilac, paper-cobalt).

**Step 2: light or dark?** If there is media, measure it: the mean OKLab L of the hero poster or first frame (the same analysis as here, run on the user's upload). L < 0.4 (night, film, studio black) → dark ground; L > 0.6 → light ground. Without media, use the purpose prior from the sample: music & events → dark (5/5 and 5/6 in our sample); institutions → mostly dark; architecture, technology, real estate, e-commerce, food → mostly light; fashion → either (3/3).

**Step 3: temperature and accent from the content.** Compute the media's dominant hue and its mean OKLab *b* (warm/cool). Warm media (wood fire, skin, sunset, food) → warm neutral (limestone, warm-black, espresso). Cool media (sea, steel, night, screens) → cool neutral (wet-concrete, charcoal-signal, night-market). Then rank accents by **hue distance from the media's dominant hue** (complement or a deliberate same-hue pick), and reject an accent that sits in the same hue band as a large media region at lower chroma (it reads as a mistake).

**Step 4: purpose and mood priors, then the look.** Score `fit = 3·mediaFit + 2·purposeFit + 2·directionFit + 1·moodFit`, where:
- `mediaFit` comes from steps 1–3 (ground zone matches, temperature matches, accent hue distance in 90–180° or < 20°);
- `purposeFit` comes from the table below;
- `directionFit` = palette is in `direction.palettes` (2) or compatible (1);
- `moodFit`: quiet/minimal → neutral + no/one accent; bold/playful/cheeky → committed ground; elegant/sophisticated → deep or warm neutral.

Only after that, a **small** "not used by the previous few recipes" bonus breaks ties. It is never the main reason.

Implementation sketch (pure, testable in `check.ts`):

```ts
type MediaStats = { L: number; warm: number; hue: number | null } // from the hero poster / photos, OKLab means
const zoneOf = (hex: string) => { const o = oklab(hex); return o.C < 0.03 ? (o.L > 0.6 ? 'light' : 'dark') : o.C >= 0.12 ? 'committed' : o.L >= 0.86 ? 'pale' : 'deep' }
export function rankPalettes(spec: RecipeSpec, media?: MediaStats) {
  const colourInMedia = ['photography', 'video', '3d'].includes(spec.lead) || !!media
  return Object.values(palettes).map((p) => {
    const z = zoneOf(p.colors.background), g = oklab(p.colors.background), a = oklab(p.colors.accent)
    let s = 0
    s += 3 * (colourInMedia ? (z === 'light' || z === 'dark' ? 1 : z === 'deep' ? 0.5 : 0) : (z === 'committed' || z === 'pale' ? 1 : 0.5))
    if (media) s += 2 * ((media.L < 0.45) === p.dark ? 1 : 0) + 1 * (Math.sign(media.warm) === Math.sign(g.b) ? 1 : 0)
    if (media?.hue != null) { const dh = Math.abs(((a.H - media.hue + 540) % 360) - 180); s += dh > 90 || dh < 20 ? 1 : 0 }
    s += 2 * (PURPOSE_FIT[spec.purpose]?.includes(p.id) ? 1 : 0) + 2 * (directions[spec.direction].palettes.includes(p.id) ? 1 : 0)
    return { id: p.id, score: s }
  }).sort((x, y) => y.score - x.score)
}
```

## 8. Caveats
- Ground/text/accent come from heuristics (DOM area weights, contrast-filtered text, chroma-filtered link colours). They are right for the large majority (spot-checked against contact sheets) but can mislabel a few sites with full-screen video over a transparent body. Per-site values are in the JSON (`ground`, `text`, `accents`, `shotTop.colors`, `shotMid.colors`) for re-checking.
- Category subsets are small (~6 per category); treat the purpose priors as directional.
- 74 picked sites were not captured (timeouts under heavy machine load, or not reached when the crawl was interrupted). The 139 kept still span every requested kind.
- Set A tunes change five palettes that built examples use (limestone → aster-house, legal-pad → inkwell-moth, pink-plaster → hane, apricot-hall → fennwood, console-lilac → halvik). Either pin their current colours as `customPalette` in their recorded spec, or rebuild them.
