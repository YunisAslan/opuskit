# Use cases: real anatomy and section variants (2026-10-04)

**Question.** What do excellent sites of each of our 18 kinds actually contain, and in how many visually different ways do they lay out
the same job? The owner feels our sections repeat. The aim is more variety and beauty with the same weight: **2–4 layout variants per
section rather than more sections.**

**Method.** About 280 candidate sites came from Awwwards industry categories (Sites of the Day, Honorable Mentions, Nominees), godly.website,
siteinspire and known award sites. Each was crawled in headed Chrome (Playwright) at 1440×900. The crawl captured the home page as scroll frames, plus one key
inner page (menu, rooms, work, pricing…) and the conversion page (book, shop, donate, enrol, contact). Every site was then judged from
its contact sheets. Dead, parked and wrong-kind sites were dropped, as were weak ones; replacements were crawled where needed.
**175 sites were kept, 8–13 per kind.** Analysts logged **395 layout variants** and **145 "missing from the kit"** items.

Raw data, including every site's home order, inner pages, conversion, memorable traits, variants and gaps, is in
`docs/research/2026-10-04-use-case-sites.json`. The screenshots were not committed; they are in the session scratchpad (`usecases/out/`).
This report builds on `2026-10-home-anatomy.md`; the default-page changes that report recommended are already in `taxonomy.ts`.

## The short version

1. **The repetition comes from one look per section, not from too few sections.** Across all 175 sites, the same ~15 section jobs make up
   most of every home page. Real sites vary the *layout* of those jobs. Distinct layouts seen per section:
   - services 30
   - featured-work 29
   - testimonials 27
   - stats 19
   - categories 19
   - pricing 18
   - location 17
   - contact-cta 16
   - reservation 16
   - team 13
   - product-grid 13
   - collection 10
   - feature-rows 10
   - product-highlight 10

   Every one of these is a single component in our kit today. Only `Footer` has variants.
2. **A `layout` prop on about 20 sections, with 3–4 values each, gives far more visible variety than any new section.** 64 concrete
   variants are proposed below, in priority order. Each one is a different arrangement, not a recolour or an effect.
3. **There are few true content gaps, but they are the pages people convert on.** These are:
   - a product **buy box** (product page)
   - a **specs** table
   - a plan **comparison** matrix
   - **tickets**
   - hotel **offers** and restaurant specials
   - nonprofit **ways to help**
   - a **project page** (portfolio, agency, studio)
   - a **property page** with an agent card
   - an **article page** body
   - an **enquiry/quote form**
   - a **changelog**
   - an experiment **gate**, sound toggle and credits

   About half of these are better as variants of existing sections (see "Variant or new section?").
4. **Defaults: three kinds need two starting anatomies, not one.** These are real-estate (agency vs development), course (online course vs
   school) and event (wedding vs festival/conference). Experiment should put the piece on the home page. Smaller fixes for every kind are in
   the defaults table.
5. **Testimonials are still over-prescribed for portfolio, studio, fashion, hotel and experiment.** There, proof is the work, press or
   rating. Where testimonials do appear, they take a *kind-specific* shape: a rating with a review carousel (restaurant, local services,
   clinic), a post wall (course), logo tabs (SaaS) or a single named story (nonprofit). This is the clearest case for variants over
   one look.

a thumbnail of it could not be mistaken for the others. The proposals are ranked by kinds served × visual difference × how often the
section is on a default page.
