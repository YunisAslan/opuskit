# Demand research: who hurts, who pays (2026-10-04)

Question: which of (A) non-developer business owners, (B) designers without code, (C) developers / freelancers /
small studios building client sites with AI agents, (D) AI website platforms licensing a "taste" engine has the
strongest, most monetisable pain?

Method: web search plus Hacker News (Algolia API) comment search, 2025–2026. Reddit could not be fetched directly,
so Reddit voices show up only second-hand. Every quote below has a link and a date. The evidence is strongest for
C and D and thinnest for A, and that gap is itself a finding.

---

## 1. What people complain about in AI-built sites

**Sameness / "AI slop" is now a named, measured thing.**

- Adrian Krebs scored 500 Show HN landing pages against 16 "AI design" patterns: Inter or Space Grotesk, purple,
  gradients, a centred hero with a badge, coloured left borders, numbered steps, shadcn, glassmorphism. **22% scored
  High (4+ patterns), 32% Medium.** "There is a difference between trying to craft your own design and just shipping
  with whatever defaults the LLMs output." "Is this bad? Not really, just uninspired."
  ([adriankrebs.ch, 2026-04-20](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/))
- Brian Douglas, "Tailwind and slop apps" (111 pts, 83 comments on HN): "the same 'look and feel' has become an
  indicator of a slop product… Prompting an LLM to 'make a stylish homepage for my product' is not a good idea.
  People can see through it instantly." ([briandouglas.ie, 2026-06-11](https://briandouglas.ie/llm-tailwind-template/))
- Adam Wathan (Tailwind) publicly apologised for making every Tailwind UI button `bg-indigo-500`, "leading to every
  AI generated UI on earth" being indigo. It got over 1M views.
  ([x.com, 2025-08-07](https://x.com/adamwathan/status/1953510802159219096))
- HN comments: "It's like those ai websites they look 'sleek' but all look the same"
  ([kelvinjps10, 2026-03-29](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47559803)); "Your site looks pretty much the same
  as every other 'AI' slop website" ([bediger4000, 2026-03-01](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47207252));
  "a bunch of responses to this post all showing off their great AI designs that are literally the same thing with
  different (each horrible) color palettes" ([adastra22, 2026-04-13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47752575));
  "AI is a floor raiser, not a ceiling raiser" ([jjfoooo4, 2026-04-13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47753109)).
- Product Hunt, "Uiverse Design – De-slop your AI generated websites" (189 upvotes, about June 2026): "The 'AI smell'
  is usually in the defaults... same hero, same gradient, same 16px everything." "The generic purple gradient with
  pill badges and emojis everywhere is basically a uniform at this point."
  ([producthunt.com](https://www.producthunt.com/p/uiverse-io/uiverse-design))
- Jeff Humble (Fountain Institute) lists 7 signs of a vibe-coded UI: neon palettes, glows, emoji icons, purple
  gradients, nested cards, rainbow side tabs, meaningless status dots. "It has no specific point of view, because
  the data it came from lacked a unified one." ([thefountaininstitute.com, 2026-04-24](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui))

**Inconsistency and craft.** "why shadows somewhere and not for other cards? why so many different font sizes with
no hierarchy? the paddings and margins are inconsistent"
([camillomiller, 2026-04-13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47753708)). "I got very frustrated with LLMs and
their inability to apply good taste or maintain consistent design languages"
([weitendorf, 2026-07-02](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48758168)).

**Editing pain and cost (builders).** Lovable's Trustpilot reviews (3.9/5, about 1,000 reviews) keep coming back to
credits burned fixing regressions, "unwanted edits and changes not requested", and unclear credit use
([trustpilot.com](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/lovable.dev?page=9)).

**SEO and reliability.** Lovable sites are client-rendered React. Crawlability and indexing problems "show up on
literally every Lovable site" ([seojuice.com, 2026](https://seojuice.com/blog/lovable-seo-tips-the-complete-checklist-2026/)).
Lovable itself published a video titled "Is Lovable AI SEO Broken?"
([lovable.dev](https://lovable.dev/video/is-lovable-ai-seo-broken-a-senior-dev-weighs-in)). A developer sourcing
templates for a client found "two bad options: expensive, locked-down ecosystems like Shopify [or] broken
AI-generated junk" ([HN Show, 2025-12-28](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46408531)).

**Counter-evidence (important).** "We only notice this 'sameness' because we're swimming in it all day... to a general
user, they just aren't seeing it as slop" ([distalx, 2026-06-12](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48499455)).
"Most UIs should be boring and effectively invisible" ([rcxdude, 2026-06-13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48515402)).
So sameness bothers builders and makers who know what they're looking at, more than it bothers end visitors. That
says who to sell to.

## 2. Freelancers and small agencies using AI agents

- Delivery time has collapsed: "reduced custom site development from around a month to less than a day", and they
  are now targeting outdated WordPress agencies ([SunshineTheCat, 2026-04-14](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47770234)).
- Prices are under pressure: "If you can do in a day what used to take a week you won't be able to charge the same"
  ([kypro, 2026-04-14](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47773004)). "Traditional web agency shops are between a
  rock and a hard place" ([phplovesong, 2026-04-05](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47646809)).
- The answer agencies are reaching for: "We've been switching to value based billing with our clients which has made
  them happier and us more profitable", using AI prototypes ([SoMomentary, 2026-04-14](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47770577)).
  Value-based billing only holds if the output looks worth more than a DIY or Lovable site. That means design quality.
- Where the time goes now is getting agents to follow a design. Work-arounds people describe: "Creating a CODING.md
  or FRONTEND.md with rules... can get you 80% of the way there"
  ([eljee, 2026-04-13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47753998)). Designing in another tool first, then making
  Claude Code screenshot its build and diff it against the mockup with ImageMagick
  ([khasan222, 2026-04-13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47754733)). Good results go to people who already have
  taste: "for Frontend, AI is great, because I know exactly what to do"
  ([mock-possum, 2026-04-13](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47761912)).
- A low-price segment is opening up: "repeatedly turning down small business owners (painters, landscapers, etc.) who
  couldn't afford $5K+ custom websites" ([goodstuart, 2025-07-20](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44621668)).
  Agents make that segment profitable for a freelancer, but only if each site still looks bespoke.
- Surveys: Freelancer.com (8,100 respondents) finds 73% of freelancers use AI. Users report saving 8+ hours a week
  and charging about 40% more per hour ([plutio.com, 2026](https://www.plutio.com/freelancer-magazine/ai-tools-for-freelancers-2026)).

Gap: no direct quotes from clients about AI-built sites. Those likely live on Reddit, which couldn't be fetched.

## 3. Non-developer business owners (A)

- UENI analysed 600 one-star Trustpilot reviews of Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy, Hostinger and UENI
  ([ueni.com, 2026-08-14](https://ueni.com/blog/website-builder-complaints/)):
  - support unreachable: **42.3%**
  - domain, DNS or email: 24.7%
  - refunds: 18%
  - "hard to use": only **3%**
  - design/look is not among the top categories.

  One review: "There is no real support, just AI repeating the same answer again and again."
- Why owners have no site: 17–27% of US small businesses have none. Cost as the reason fell from 26% (2018) to 16%
  (2025). The top reason is now "not relevant to my industry", and 21% say their social page *is* their website
  ([review42 roundup of Clutch and others](https://resources.review42.com/what-percentage-of-small-businesses-have-a-website/)).
- What owners want: a clear offer, proof, and one call to action (call, book, quote), plus working bookings and forms.
  Builders let them down on support, domains, billing and being found. Uniqueness is rarely their complaint.

So owners feel the pain of being unattended (no support, DNS, SEO), not the pain of a look that isn't distinctive.
They pay a little and churn a lot.

What this says:
1. People pay for *taste packaged as a starting point*. Framer is the healthy proof.
2. Static kits sold through docs are collapsing, because agents bypass the storefront (Tailwind). Value has to be
   delivered *inside the agent's workflow*.
3. The anti-slop *prompt* layer is already free and popular. Rules alone won't sell. What isn't free is a resolved
   direction plus verified, ready code and QA.

  - **Design Systems** on every paid plan ([2026-08-19](https://www.createwith.com/tool/lovable/updates/lovable-design-systems-bring-brand-consistency-to-every-paid-plan))

- **Acquisitions:**
  - Google bought Galileo AI (text-to-UI), which became Stitch ([May 2025](https://uxmagic.ai/blog/galileo-ai-is-now-google-stitch))

teams. Licensing a taste engine to them is a plausible exit or partnership, not a first market.

---

## Verdict: C first, with B close by. D later. A is not the buyer.

**C (freelancers / small studios using agents) has the strongest pain you can charge for:**
- Their pain is economic, not aesthetic. AI shrank a month to a day, so prices fall unless the work looks clearly
  better than DIY. Distinctiveness is how they protect their margin.
- They already run Claude Code or Cursor. OpusKit's output (a Build Package for an agent) is their native format.
  That avoids Tailwind's mistake of selling through docs that agents bypass.
- They already pay for this category: templates, Relume, kits, and Framer licences per client. A per-site or
  monthly price fits how they already buy.
- They build many sites, so they come back with every new client.

**B (designers who vibe-code)** is a growing, adjacent group: Claude is designers' #2 weekly tool (50.8%) and Claude
Code is #4. 43.8% spend more than half their time vibe coding, and 59.1% built a tool with AI in the last six months
([UX Tools State of Prototyping, Spring 2026, n=1,478](https://www.uxtools.co/survey/2026/state-of-prototyping)).
They already have taste. What they need is for it to survive into code, which is Framer's market and pays well.
Good second segment, and the most likely to rave about OpusKit.

**D** is real but the platforms are building their own (Lovable Aesthetics and Design Systems). Pursue it after C
proves the engine. **A** complains about support, domains and SEO, not looks, and churns. Serve owners *through* C.

## Top 5 jobs to be done

1. **"Make it not look AI-made."** A committed, specific direction before any code: palette, type, layout, motion.
   *"the same 'look and feel' has become an indicator of a slop product"* (Douglas, 2026-06-11). *"literally the same
   thing with different… color palettes"* (adastra22, 2026-04-13). 22% of Show HN pages match 4+ slop patterns
   (Krebs, 2026-04-20).
2. **"Give my agent a design it will actually follow, across every page."** Tokens, a section library and rules, all
   loaded into the agent. *"inability to apply good taste or maintain consistent design languages"* (weitendorf,
   2026-07-02). *"FRONTEND.md… 80% of the way there"* (eljee). Lovable shipping Design Systems shows the demand
   (2026-08-19).
3. **"Deliver a client site in a day that still justifies an agency price."** *"month to less than a day"*
   (SunshineTheCat). *"won't be able to charge the same"* (kypro). *"value based billing… happier and us more
   profitable"* (SoMomentary), all from 2026-04-14.
4. **"Show the client what they'll get, and let them choose, before building."** Real example sites and options side
   by side to anchor a value-based quote. *"switching to value based billing… using AI prototypes"* (SoMomentary).
   Lovable's Aesthetics lets people pick typography, layout and colour up front (2026-05-11). Webflow's AI output is
   "a first draft" only (appsrow, 2026).
5. **"Ship production basics I don't have to re-check."** Static/SSR for SEO, accessibility, mobile, real media,
   consistent spacing, plus a QA checklist. *"broken AI-generated junk"* (HN, 2025-12-28). Crawl problems on
   "literally every Lovable site" (seojuice, 2026). *"paddings and margins are inconsistent"* (camillomiller,
   2026-04-13).

Next research step: about ten interviews with freelancers and small studios who already ship client sites with
Claude Code or Cursor. Test willingness to pay per site versus per month, and whether showing "what kind of site
you'll get" closes clients faster.
